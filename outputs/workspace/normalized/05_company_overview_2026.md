1. Company profile
Lumivaara Logistics Oy is a Finnish family-owned third-party logistics (3PL) company headquartered
in Vantaa. The company provides contract warehousing, domestic road freight, e-commerce
fulfilment, customs clearance and value-added logistics services to business customers across
Finland. Operations are run from three warehouses in Vantaa, Tampere and Oulu with a combined
warehouse area of 33,500 m².
The company employs 134 people (full-time equivalents at the end of 2025), complemented by
temporary agency staff during peak seasons. Revenue in 2025 was EUR 24.6 million. Lumivaara's
value proposition is “reliable logistics with a personal touch”: customers have a named contact
person, and the company is known for flexible handling of irregular volumes and special requests.
Mission: We make our customers' supply chains work every day, so they can focus on their own
business.
Vision 2028: The most reliable and efficient mid-sized logistics partner in Finland, growing
profitably through smart use of technology.

3. Services
3.1 Contract warehousing
Storage and handling of customers' goods in ambient, chilled (+2…+8 °C) and GDP-controlled (+15…
+25 °C) conditions. Services include receiving, quality checks, putaway, inventory management,
picking and packing, and dispatch. Typical contracts run for three years with a monthly fee per pallet
position and per-transaction handling fees.
3.2 Domestic road freight
Full-truckload (FTL) and less-than-truckload (LTL) transport throughout Finland, with daily distribution
routes in the Helsinki metropolitan area, Pirkanmaa and Northern Ostrobothnia. Linehaul between
the three warehouses runs every weekday night. About 55 % of transport volume is run with the
company's own fleet and 45 % with subcontracted hauliers.


[Page 4]
3.3 E-commerce fulfilment
Order fulfilment for online retailers: integration with web shops, same-day dispatch for orders
received by 15:00, consumer returns handling, and parcel carrier hand-over. E-commerce volumes are
highly seasonal; November and December typically account for 28 % of annual e-commerce order
lines.
3.4 Customs clearance
Import and export declarations for customers importing from outside the EU, mainly from Asia and
the United Kingdom. Declarations are prepared by two licensed customs specialists using the Finnish
Customs electronic service.
3.5 Value-added services
Labelling, kitting and assembly of product sets, repacking, returns inspection and campaign display
building for retail customers.

9. Core process descriptions
9.1 Inbound (receiving)
1. The customer sends an advance shipping notice (ASN) by EDI, email or the customer portal.
About 35 % of inbound deliveries arrive without an ASN.
2. The truck arrives at the gate; the gate clerk checks the CMR waybill and assigns an unloading
door.
3. Warehouse workers unload, count and inspect the goods, and record deviations (damaged
pallets, shortages) on paper and in the WMS.
4. Goods are labelled and put away to locations suggested by the WMS.
5. The signed CMR is scanned at the end of the shift and filed in the document archive.
9.2 Outbound (picking and dispatch)
1. Customer orders arrive by EDI (62 % of order lines), web shop integration (27 %) or email/Excel
(11 %). Email and Excel orders are keyed in manually by customer service.
2. The WMS releases picking waves; workers pick with handheld scanners.
3. Goods are packed, labelled and staged; the WMS creates the shipment and hands it over to
the TMS.
4. Traffic planners assign shipments to routes or subcontractors in the TMS.
5. The driver confirms the delivery in the mobile app, collects a signature and photographs the
POD if a paper document is used.
9.3 Freight document handling
Freight documents include CMR waybills, delivery notes, proof-of-delivery (POD) documents, packing
lists and customs documents. Documents arrive by email (PDF), as photos from the driver app and
subcontractors, and on paper at the warehouse gates. Document clerks open each document, find
the matching shipment in the TMS and key in the delivery confirmation, package and weight data,
pallet exchange information and any remarks. Invoicing-relevant data is also entered in Business
Central.
The process is fully manual. Documents from subcontractors arrive with a delay of 2–10 days, and
invoicing of a shipment cannot be completed until its POD has been processed. Remarks about
damage or shortages written by hand on the documents are often noticed late, after the deadline for
raising a claim against the haulier (7 days under the company's subcontracting terms).
9.4 Customer service
Customer service handles a shared mailbox and a phone line. Typical requests are shipment status
enquiries, changes to bookings, delivery time requests, damage reports and claims, invoicing
questions and quote requests. Agents look up shipment status manually in the TMS and the WMS.


[Page 10]
There is no ticketing system; follow-up of open cases relies on email flags and personal notes. The
target is to respond to all emails within four working hours.
9.5 Workforce planning
Shift supervisors plan staffing one week ahead using their experience, the previous weeks' volumes
and the customer forecasts available. Only about a third of the key customers provide regular volume
forecasts. Short-term gaps are covered with overtime or agency workers booked the previous
afternoon at a premium price.

11. Strategy 2026–2028 and digitalisation goals
The board approved an updated strategy in June 2026. The strategy has four focus areas:
1. Profitable growth – grow revenue to EUR 30 million by 2028, especially in pharma/healthcare
and industrial spare parts, while improving the EBIT margin to 5 %.
2. Operational excellence – reduce manual work in office processes by 30 % by the end of 2028
so that growth does not require additional office staff.
3. Customer experience – real-time visibility and faster answers for customers; NPS +45 by 2028.
4. People – reduce warehouse staff turnover and improve planning so that overtime is cut by a
third.
11.1 Digitalisation and AI goals
Map core processes and identify opportunities for automation and artificial intelligence (in
progress, autumn 2026).
Run at least one AI pilot in 2027 with a measurable business case.
Introduce an AI usage policy and training for office staff.
Improve data quality: shared customer master data and a common reporting database by
2028.
Evaluate a ticketing system for customer service.
Budget: The 2027 budget proposal earmarks EUR 50,000 for digitalisation and AI pilots, in
addition to ordinary IT operating costs (approx. EUR 210,000 per year). Further investment will be
decided based on pilot results.
12. Data protection and information security
Lumivaara processes personal data of customer contact persons, its own employees and drivers, and
consumers (recipients of e-commerce deliveries: names, addresses, phone numbers, sometimes
delivery instructions). The company acts as a data processor for its e-commerce customers under
data processing agreements. A privacy statement and a record of processing activities exist; the CFO
acts as the data protection contact person. No data protection officer has been appointed.
Information security is managed together with an IT outsourcing partner: multi-factor authentication
for Microsoft 365, endpoint protection, and backups of the on-premise WMS. The company has no
written policy on the use of generative AI tools. Several customer contracts (especially in pharma)
include clauses restricting the transfer of customer data outside the EU/EEA.
