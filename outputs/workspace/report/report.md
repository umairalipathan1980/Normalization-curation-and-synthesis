## 1. Executive Summary

Lumivaara Logistics Oy is a Finnish family-owned third-party logistics company with an AI maturity level preliminarily assessed by FAIR as Low: its digital operations are established, but AI use is individual and there is no AI strategy, policy or roadmap (company_overview_2026.pdf, page 2; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “2. CURRENT USE OF AI AND DIGITAL TOOLS”). Its top-priority use case is freight document processing, where AI would extract document fields into the TMS, reducing manual data entry and related errors, while flagging handwritten damage remarks to support timely claims; uncertain results would go to staff for review (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “3.1 Freight document processing (highest priority for the company)”; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3). The main recommendation is to begin with a proof of concept for proof-of-delivery documents at the Vantaa site, testing on real documents, including poor-quality images and handwriting, and comparing extraction accuracy with clerk entries (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; process_map_freight_documents.png).

## 3. AI Maturity Level

The company has a WMS, a TMS and EDI connections with key customers. AI use is individual, with no AI strategy, policy or roadmap. As a spring 2026 pilot, the company purchased Microsoft 365 Copilot licences for 25 office staff; use is uneven, with some employees using it daily for emails and meeting summaries and others not using it. Several employees also use free ChatGPT for translations and drafting customer letters, raising concern about customer data being entered into public tools. The company has no machine-learning models in production. Its UiPath bot, implemented in 2023, matches supplier invoices to purchase orders in Business Central and handles about 70% of invoices automatically; this is rule-based automation, not AI. The IT function consists of one person with SQL and Power BI skills but no Python or machine-learning experience (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “2. CURRENT USE OF AI AND DIGITAL TOOLS” and “5. TECHNICAL CAPABILITY AND RESOURCES”).

The company cites rising wages and fuel costs, price pressure from large retail customers and a goal to grow without hiring more office staff for each new customer as reasons for pursuing AI. Its strategy also sets a goal of reducing manual office work by 30% by 2028 (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3). Unresolved: the CEO said the board would be open to investing around EUR 60,000–80,000 in AI-related projects in 2027 if the first pilot shows results, while the strategy document’s 2027 budget proposal earmarks EUR 50,000 for digitalisation and AI pilots, with further investment dependent on pilot results (advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3; company_overview_2026.pdf, page 11). ## 6. Validity of Concept and Authenticity of Problem Addressed

The freight-document problem is supported by evidence of substantial manual effort and measurable errors. Six clerks enter document information into the transport system, and invoicing fields are entered again in Business Central. Finding a shipment takes about one minute, and data entry takes a further three to four minutes, depending on the document. An estimate from the meeting put entry errors at about 4%; the errors can result in incorrect invoices, credit notes and customer disputes, with finance staff spending about one day a week correcting them (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3). Unresolved: one meeting estimate was about 1,200 freight documents per day across all sites, while another was around 1,000 on a normal working day according to the TMS log, which counts paper documents when scanned (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3). The company processed 71% of proof-of-delivery documents within two days in 2025, against a 95% target (company_overview_2026.pdf, page 10). For Vantaa in September 2026, the operations extract records 15,126 documents, 724.6 hours of manual entry and 616 documents with a later correction in TMS or Business Central (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet Monthly_Volumes, row 98).

Freight-document processing is the concept closest to implementation: the advisors identified it as the best first proof of concept because of its volume and measurable baseline. They proposed testing proof-of-delivery documents at the Vantaa site first, using real documents, including poor-quality photos and handwritten remarks, and comparing field-level extraction accuracy with clerk entries. The proposed workflow would send uncertain documents for human review (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3).

## 7. Integration and Importance of AI in the Idea

The advisors considered freight document processing the best candidate for a first proof of concept (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3). Six clerks currently enter document information manually into the TMS, and enter invoicing-related fields into Business Central (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 3.1 Freight document processing (highest priority for the company)). The proposed system would use a vision-language model to read documents, extract fields and send them to the TMS through its API. Advisors proposed testing it on real documents, including poor-quality photos and handwritten remarks, and comparing field-level accuracy with clerk entries (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; process_map_freight_documents.png). Handwriting was identified as the main risk to test (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 7. ADVISORS' OBSERVATIONS (internal, Elina & Daniel)). The intended workflow keeps a person involved to check uncertain results, while the system should flag damage remarks promptly to support same-day claims (advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3). AI is central to the proposed automation of document reading and data entry, with human review focused on uncertain cases (advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3; advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3).

For warehouse staffing, the proposed approach is time-series machine learning, rather than generative AI, to forecast labour needs (advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3). Supervisors currently plan staffing weekly using their experience, the previous week’s volumes and customer forecasts when available; the proposed first step is to assess the WMS data (advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3; advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3).

For customer service, the proposed AI workflow would classify incoming emails, route them, retrieve shipment status from the TMS and draft replies. Agents would approve every answer, at least initially, so the replies are not intended to be fully automatic (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3). AI would support the agent’s work, while approval remains with a person (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3).

Other ideas raised were route optimisation, predictive maintenance for trucks and automatic customs declarations (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3). The route-planning issue discussed was that planners override the TMS when it lacks customers’ actual unloading times; a vendor update might be available in 2027 (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3). Predictive maintenance was mentioned alongside the availability of telematics data (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3). Automatic customs declarations were mentioned, but Kaisa considered them too risky for now (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3).

## 15. Recommendations

- Start the first proof of concept with freight document processing, scoped to proof-of-delivery documents at the Vantaa site. Expand to other document types or sites only if the pilot works (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “7. ADVISORS' OBSERVATIONS (internal, Elina & Daniel)”).

- Test a vision-language model on real documents, including poor-quality photos and handwritten remarks. Measure accuracy at field level against the information entered by clerks; handwriting was identified as the main risk to test (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “7. ADVISORS' OBSERVATIONS (internal, Elina & Daniel)”).

- Prepare an anonymized sample of 200 freight documents across document types, sites and languages, including at least 50 with handwritten remarks. Include the clerks’ entries for comparison if available. The action list gives 30 October 2026 as the deadline (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “8. ACTION ITEMS”).

- Export daily WMS volumes by site for 2020–2026 and the 2025 TMS document log by 30 October 2026. Conduct a one-week time study in Vantaa to record current clerk time for each document type (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “8. ACTION ITEMS”; advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3).

- Keep human review in the document workflow: have the model assign a confidence level and send uncertain documents to a person for checking. For the pilot, a record-by-record API to the TMS was considered sufficient (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; process_map_freight_documents.png; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3).

- Introduce an AI usage policy and short practical Copilot training regardless of whether the pilots proceed. Treat email triage as a later step, after the policy is in place, because it is technically straightforward but has the greatest compliance workload (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “7. ADVISORS' OBSERVATIONS (internal, Elina & Daniel)”).

- Choose a low-maintenance solution or one operated by a partner, given the company’s single-person IT function. Do not pursue automatic customs declarations for now, as they were considered too risky (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “7. ADVISORS' OBSERVATIONS (internal, Elina & Daniel)”).
