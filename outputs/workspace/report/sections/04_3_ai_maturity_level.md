## 3. AI Maturity Level

The company has a WMS, a TMS and EDI connections with key customers. AI use is individual, with no AI strategy, policy or roadmap. As a spring 2026 pilot, the company purchased Microsoft 365 Copilot licences for 25 office staff; use is uneven, with some employees using it daily for emails and meeting summaries and others not using it. Several employees also use free ChatGPT for translations and drafting customer letters, raising concern about customer data being entered into public tools. The company has no machine-learning models in production. Its UiPath bot, implemented in 2023, matches supplier invoices to purchase orders in Business Central and handles about 70% of invoices automatically; this is rule-based automation, not AI. The IT function consists of one person with SQL and Power BI skills but no Python or machine-learning experience (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “2. CURRENT USE OF AI AND DIGITAL TOOLS” and “5. TECHNICAL CAPABILITY AND RESOURCES”).

The company cites rising wages and fuel costs, price pressure from large retail customers and a goal to grow without hiring more office staff for each new customer as reasons for pursuing AI. Its strategy also sets a goal of reducing manual office work by 30% by 2028 (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3). Unresolved: the CEO said the board would be open to investing around EUR 60,000–80,000 in AI-related projects in 2027 if the first pilot shows results, while the strategy document’s 2027 budget proposal earmarks EUR 50,000 for digitalisation and AI pilots, with further investment dependent on pilot results (advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3; company_overview_2026.pdf, page 11). FAIR’s preliminary assessment rates the company’s AI maturity as Low: operational digitalisation is strong, but AI use remains individual and ad hoc, with no AI strategy, policy or roadmap and no AI-specific skills in-house (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “2. CURRENT USE OF AI AND DIGITAL TOOLS”).
