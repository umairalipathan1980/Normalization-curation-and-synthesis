## 4. Current Solution Development Stage

The company’s AI assessment was in progress in autumn 2026, and it had no machine learning models in production. It had purchased Microsoft Copilot licences for 25 office staff as a pilot. The strategy sets a goal of running at least one AI pilot with a measurable business case in 2027, reducing manual work in office processes by 30% by the end of 2028, and introducing an AI usage policy and training for office staff. (company_overview_2026.pdf, page 11; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 2. CURRENT USE OF AI AND DIGITAL TOOLS; advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3)

Freight document processing is identified as the company’s highest-priority use case. Documents arrive as email attachments, through the driver app and on paper at warehouse gates. Six document clerks manually enter data into the TMS and invoicing-relevant fields into Business Central. The fields include shipment and customer references, sender and receiver details, package and weight information, pallet details, delivery date and time, signature status and remarks. (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 3.1 Freight document processing (highest priority for the company)) Unresolved: The daily document volume estimates differ: one estimate is around 1,200 across all sites, while another is around 1,000 on a normal working day according to the TMS log, which counts paper documents when scanned. (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3) The estimated manual-entry error rate is around 4%; errors lead to invoice corrections and disputes, and finance spends about a day a week on corrections. Handwritten damage remarks on proof-of-delivery documents can be missed, delaying claims and sometimes causing the company to lose the right to claim against the haulier. (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 3.1 Freight document processing (highest priority for the company)) The objective is to extract fields automatically into the TMS, send low-confidence documents for human review and flag handwritten remarks. The proposed first proof of concept would focus on proof-of-delivery documents at the Vantaa site, testing a vision language model on real documents, including poor-quality photos and handwritten remarks, and comparing field-level accuracy with clerk entries. (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 3.1 Freight document processing (highest priority for the company); advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 7. Other use cases identified include warehouse labour forecasting, customer service email triage, route planning, predictive maintenance for trucks and automatic customs declarations. Short-term warehouse staffing gaps are currently covered by overtime or agency workers booked the previous afternoon at a premium. The proposed forecasting use case would predict daily inbound pallets and outbound order lines per warehouse one to three weeks ahead. (company_overview_2026.pdf, page 10; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 3.2 Warehouse labour demand forecasting) The seven-person customer service team handles a shared mailbox receiving roughly 380 emails a day, rising to over 600 in December; about half concern shipment tracking. Against a four-working-hour response target, average response time in peak periods is around nine hours. The proposed tool would classify and route emails, look up shipment status in the TMS and draft replies for an agent to approve; fully automatic replies are not intended at the outset. (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 3.3 Customer service email triage and reply drafting) Planners use the TMS route-planning module for Vantaa-area distribution but often override it because it does not know customers’ unloading times; a vendor update may be available in 2027. Predictive maintenance for trucks was raised as an idea, with telematics data available, while the fleet manager was not present. Automatic customs declarations were also mentioned, but were considered too risky for now. (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, 2. CURRENT USE OF AI AND DIGITAL TOOLS; advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3)
