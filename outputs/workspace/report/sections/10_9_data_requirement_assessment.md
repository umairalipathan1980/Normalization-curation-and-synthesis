## 9. Data Requirement Assessment

Freight-document processing requires shipment and customer references, sender and receiver details, package and weight information, pallet details, delivery date and time, signature status, and remarks such as damage or shortages. Documents arrive as email PDFs, driver-app photos and paper documents at warehouse gates, with paper scanned in batches. They cover Finnish, Swedish, English and Estonian, as well as some Polish and Lithuanian, and their image quality varies, including night-time photos taken in truck cabins (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “3.1 Freight document processing (highest priority for the company)”). The archive contains an estimated 1.5 million scanned PDFs since 2019, organized by site and month on a file server, without indexing or search. The inventory describes about 1.5 million image-only PDFs totalling 2.3 TB, with inconsistent filenames and mixed quality (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “4. DATA AND SYSTEMS”; operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet Data_Inventory, row 5). The TMS document log has about 1.5 million registered entries since June 2019 and can be exported as a CSV report of up to 100,000 rows; paper documents are registered on the scan date. The TMS API operates one record at a time, and bulk export is a separately paid service (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet Data_Inventory, row 4; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3). The driver app holds about 400,000 POD photos and signatures in the TMS, with reported issues including night photos, blur and partial documents (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet Data_Inventory, row 6). A sample contains 150 freight documents logged during a one-week time study across Vantaa, Tampere and Oulu from 28 September to 2 October 2026 (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet README, row 8). **Unresolved:** daily volume is estimated at about 1,200 documents across all sites in the part 1 recording and meeting notes, while the part 2 recording gives around 1,000 on a normal working day based on the TMS log. The latter estimate is affected by paper documents being counted when scanned (advisory_meeting_recording_part1.mp3; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “3.1 Freight document processing (highest priority for the company)”; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3).

The proposed warehouse labour forecast would use WMS history and customer forecasts to predict daily inbound pallets and outbound order lines per warehouse one to three weeks ahead. Calendar effects, including holidays and campaigns, and possibly weather for chilled-food customers are additional candidate inputs (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “3.2 Warehouse labour demand forecasting”). The WMS has about 48 million transaction rows in its current format from 2020, covering receipts, putaways, picks, shipments and inventory movements; it is held in on-premise SQL Server tables, with read-only SQL access for IT. Data before the 2020 upgrade is in an older structure and is not directly comparable, and pallet types are inconsistent (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet Data_Inventory, row 2; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3; company_overview_2026.pdf, page 8; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “4. DATA AND SYSTEMS”). Oulu’s WMS data starts when the site opened in 2023, while the operational extract includes only 2024 onwards (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet README, row 16). Customer forecasts are weekly Excel attachments received by email; they are irregular, use different formats and are often missing. About a third of key customers provide forecasts regularly, and the inventory lists approximately 12 customers as sources (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet Data_Inventory, row 10; advisory_meeting_recording_part2.mp3).

The proposed customer-service email use case would classify incoming emails, route them, retrieve shipment status from the TMS and draft replies for an agent to approve (advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3). The shared Outlook mailbox contains about 300,000 emails from January 2021, accessible through Microsoft Graph, but categories are not stored. Emails may contain consumer names, phone numbers and home delivery addresses (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet Data_Inventory, row 8; advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3). Several customer contracts, especially in pharma, restrict transfers of customer data outside the EU/EEA (company_overview_2026.pdf, page 11). The operational extract also includes a customer-service category tally for one week per month from April to September 2026 (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet README, row 9).

Other identified data needs and gaps include customer unloading times for route planning: the TMS route-planning module does not use actual unloading times, and planners often override its plans for this reason (advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “2. CURRENT USE OF AI AND DIGITAL TOOLS”). Telematics data for the truck predictive-maintenance idea includes vehicle position, fuel and driving events, with about 120 million position points available by CSV export, but it is not linked to shipments; the fleet manager was not at the meeting (operations_data_extract.xlsx, sheet Data_Inventory, row 9; advisory_meeting_recording_part3.mp3). Across systems, customer master data is maintained separately in the WMS, TMS and Business Central, with no shared customer identifier. The company has no central data warehouse and relies on manual Excel exports for reporting; data-owner roles are not defined (company_overview_2026.pdf, page 8; advisory_meeting_notes.txt, “4. DATA AND SYSTEMS”).
